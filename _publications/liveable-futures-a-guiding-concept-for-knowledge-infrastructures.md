Liveable futures bring into question the conditions that make human and more-than-human lives possible, and that provide these with prospects. This collectively written article proposes “liveable futures” as a guiding concept for knowledge infrastructures, which by creating and recreating objects, categories and relations shape what forms of life become intelligible and what forms remain precarious. The authors examine why two dominant labels fall short. Sustainability is too closely tied to development and growth to orient action beyond the preservation of vested interests, and its economic logic sets industry apart from society rather than within it. The Anthropocene, meanwhile, foregrounds the interaction of the social and the natural but lumps humanity into a single actor, erasing differences in responsibility and vulnerability and placing agency at a scale that is hard to imagine. Against both, liveability is offered as a humbler and more accessible starting point, one that invites the question “what is a liveable future for you, with others?” rather than “what to do about the Anthropocene?” The plural form insists that the task is not to find the single best possible future but to open space for different ways of achieving liveability, adapted to rich localities. Emerging from the Knowledge Infrastructures Department and the faculty of Campus Fryslân at the University of Groningen, the concept also grounds an annual Liveable Futures Festival and a book series with Amsterdam University Press.
